About FDQ

Quality that holds up in the field, not just in the binder.

Why it exists

I've reviewed more quality plans than I can count. Most of them were written to pass an audit, not to help a crew do the work right.

I've been on jobs where the quality program had a procedure for everything and nobody in the field had read any of it. Ask a hand where to find the answer to a basic question about their scope and you'd watch them flip through a binder with no idea where to look.

That kind of program costs money, slows the crew down and still lets problems through. The rework that hurts a pipeline job is a cut-out weld, a coating repair or a re-shot tie-in, and each one costs days. The people who stop it are the ones standing at the joint, and the best quality hands I've worked with came up through the crew.

What FDQ is

FDQ is a certification in three tiers for pipeline and station construction. Tier 1 opens first. It takes a hand with field time and no quality background into a first job as a contractor QC tech or an owner-side junior inspector.

Tier 2, for working QC inspectors and quality engineers, and Tier 3, for the managers who build a quality program, are coming later.

How the courses are built

Every Tier 1 lesson is written against a list of things a graduate can do, and every test question checks one of them. If a lesson doesn't teach something on the list, it gets cut.

Tier 1 teaches you to recognize a problem and record it. It says plainly where the CWI, API 1169, AMPP or NDE technician takes over, and it never teaches you to sign off work that needs their cert.

Who's behind it

I started as a field inspector, holding the weld log on the right-of-way, and I've worked quality on pipeline, LNG, compressor station and industrial jobs since. FDQ is built from what I watched hold up on those jobs and what didn't.
